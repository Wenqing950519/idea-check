# Methodology

## 1. Decide now

The student is choosing now, not preparing for another interview. Produce a current `take`, `conditional_take`, or `decline` verdict from existing evidence. Never defer the decision, ask the student to return to the employer, or make the outcome contingent on a future answer.

Use the next 3–6 months as the unit of analysis:

1. What work is most likely to happen?
2. What durable outcomes can the student carry away?
3. Is that Base Case return worth the time and real alternatives?

Input level changes confidence and precision, not whether a decision is allowed. With only Company + Title, decide conservatively with low confidence. With JD, reconstruct likely work. Interview and Offer material refine the estimate; none are a permission gate.

## 2. Separate Base Case from upside

Classify each material claim:

| Class | Treatment |
| --- | --- |
| Confirmed | Include in Base Case. |
| Plausible but unverified | Mention as upside only. Do not raise formal scores. |
| Unknown | Lower confidence. Where a high score needs that proof, use a conservative middle-to-low assumption. |
| Negative Evidence | Include directly in Base Case risks. |

Do not convert ordinary sparse public data into a red flag. Also do not award value for an attractive but unsupported claim. “Unknown mentor” means mentorship is not evidenced; it does not mean the mentor does not exist, but it cannot support a high learning score.

## 3. Reconstruct actual work

Translate JD and interview language into observable work. For each likely task, identify:

- recurring action;
- deliverable;
- user or stakeholder;
- tools and methods;
- decision authority;
- feedback source and cadence, if evidenced;
- success measure;
- confidence and evidence IDs.

Do not copy phrases such as “assist growth,” “participate in AI,” or “gain end-to-end exposure.” If work may split between high-value and repetitive tasks, state the Base Case mix or scenario. Flag work degradation when the role may become capacity filling, manual QA without ownership, unstructured founder errands, generic content production, or full-time output without support.

## 4. Test proximity to core business

Classify the role as:

- `direct`: builds, sells, delivers, or improves the core product/service and has feedback from the value or revenue loop;
- `adjacent`: supports a core team and can see meaningful inputs and outcomes, but does not own the loop;
- `support`: internal, peripheral, or easily detached from customer/product outcomes;
- `unclear`: evidence cannot locate the work.

Core proximity helps only when the student can retain evidence of contribution. Broad founder exposure or many tasks do not prove high-quality learning.

## 5. Work backward from portable outcomes

Start this section with **實習結束後可能寫在履歷上的三個經驗**. Write exactly three experiences, each with:

- a conservative resume bullet that does not overstate ownership;
- **可量化線索:** facts the student can personally retain during the work, such as number of briefs, campaigns, stakeholders, workflow iterations, turnaround time, adoption, quality checks, or defensible outcome data;
- **可偷師的方法:** a repeatable practice to observe and learn, such as brief decomposition, research framing, review logic, stakeholder handoff, experiment design, or AI workflow construction;
- a Base Case boundary when ownership, attribution, metrics, or portfolio rights are not evidenced.

Do not invent metrics. When the role does not evidence access to business KPIs, point to process-level evidence the student can truthfully retain, and label it as a clue rather than a promised result. These three experiences describe likely portable outcomes; they must not raise the current Offer verdict above the Base Case.

Estimate the Base Case across:

- **能力:** methods the student can repeat elsewhere;
- **專案:** bounded work with a problem, action, and outcome;
- **數據:** measurable change, throughput, quality, adoption, revenue, cost, or learning metric;
- **作品:** code, design, analysis, campaign, playbook, research, or sanitized case study;
- **履歷成果:** defensible outcomes the student can explain.

Do not promise confidential artifacts or metrics. If portfolio rights or attribution are unknown, do not count them as formal upside; state the conservative assumption in **主要決策不確定性**.

## 6. Apply the three lenses

### Organizational Coherence

Check decision-relevant consistency among company name, legal/brand identity, founders, location, team size, product, and recruiting story. Small-company scarcity is normal. Escalate only when conflicts or missing expected traces change trust in the role.

### Labor Model

Infer how the company scopes junior labor. Consider repeat intern roles, contractor dependence, unpaid/commission-heavy work, recurring short placements, and whether roles have bounded outcomes or fill capacity gaps.

### Autonomy ≠ Mentorship

Assess independently:

- **Ownership:** defined result and decision scope;
- **Feedback:** prompt, specific review;
- **Mentorship:** capable person invested in development;
- **Methodology:** standards, examples, and repeatable ways to improve.

High autonomy with weak feedback is output pressure, not automatically learning.

## 7. Research only decision-changing claims

For each search, state internally which claim it tests and how it could change the current decision. Prefer direct sources: public product, customers, partner pages, public projects, named manager track record, hiring patterns, and official records.

Stop when evidence supports, contradicts, or leaves the claim unknown without likely decision impact. Do not default to court, financial, director, or regulator searches. Escalate only when a concrete signal makes the result relevant to the student’s actual 3–6 months.

## 8. Evaluate downside and opportunity cost

Assess student-facing loss:

- scope drifts into repetitive or unrelated work;
- no qualified reviewer corrects work;
- feedback rewards volume rather than quality;
- outcomes cannot be finished, measured, shown, or explained;
- management churn resets scope;
- schedule, commute, pay, or workload crowds out coursework and stronger options;
- a known alternative is materially stronger.

Compare with real alternatives only. If alternatives are unknown, do not invent an ideal option; make the current decision with lower confidence and say the Base Case does not include a superior alternative.

## 9. State the current verdict

Use five scores to visualize reasoning, never as a spreadsheet formula. The formal verdict is driven by the Base Case. Best / Base / Worst cases may appear, but Best Case never lifts the formal recommendation above what present evidence supports.

- `take`: Base Case is a strong use of the student’s available 3–6 months.
- `conditional_take`: Base Case has real value, but known tradeoffs, downside, or opportunity-cost sensitivity keep it below a clear take.
- `decline`: Base Case return is too weak or risk dominates.

Describe evidence gaps only under **主要決策不確定性** using: missing evidence → score effect → conservative Base Case assumption. Do not phrase entries as questions and do not prescribe follow-up with the employer.
